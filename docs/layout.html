d2 layout: which d1 ring is in which group

The options are same, alternate, square and triangle. Rings are drawn at their real place on the clock (ring r at r:30).

No layout matches this balance and clustering.

same: all d1 rings alike

No grouping at the d2 level: all 12 d1 rings get identical effects. The groups are made inside each d1 ring instead, on its pixels through segments and position functions (described later).

alternate

Groups alternate around the d2 ring in blocks of k rings, cycling through all G groups: ring r is in group floor((r - 1) / k) mod G. At lower balance, 2-group alternate becomes uneven: B gets the last block of every 3 (mid) or every 4 (least). Combinations where k × G does not divide 12 would leave a seam between ring 12 and ring 1, so they are not supported.

square: rings 1, 4, 7, 10 together

Rings 1, 4, 7 and 10 sit 90° apart (1:30, 4:30, 7:30, 10:30), so as one group they form a symmetric square. The other 8 rings are the four pairs between them: 2-3 (right), 5-6 (bottom), 8-9 (left) and 11-12 (top).

triangle: rings 1, 5, 9 together

Rings 1, 5 and 9 sit 120° apart (1:30, 5:30, 9:30), so as one group they form a symmetric triangle. It is the least balanced d2 layout.

combined: a d2 split, then a d1 split

A balanced 2-group d2 layout splits the d1 rings into X (its group A) and Y, and a d1 layout splits them further. 2 groups: checker (Y gets the d1 split inverted, still balanced) or accent in X (only X is split and Y stays A, so A = 3/4). 3 groups: X splits into A and B, Y is C. 4 groups: X into A and B, Y into C and D. The pairs closest to the clustering value are shown (a high value favors the d2 split, a low value the d1 split).

d1 layout: which small ring is in which group

The same idea inside one d1 ring: its 12 small rings (d0 0-11) are split into 2 groups (only 2 are supported). Drawn on the d1 ring's own clock, with local 12 facing the d2 center and d0 i at local i:30. Each card shows the segment and position function to give the effect: ind (small ring i has relPos = i / 12) for whole small rings, and centric (pixel p of every small ring has relPos = p / 12) for the d0 layouts, rand (a random relPos per pixel) for the random split, and arc / updown for the shapes those segments give. The function returns 0 for group A and 1 for group B; the colors on this page are computed by evaluating that function, the same way the renderer does.
